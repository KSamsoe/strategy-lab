Find something that works on a broad universe WITHOUT a tailwind. Every previous
session on this platform ran on 21 megacap technology names over 2021-2026, and
that turned out to be the whole story. Read the next paragraph carefully, because
it is the reason this session exists.

WHAT THE LAST WINNER ACTUALLY WAS. A strategy called blend_tilt (SPY core plus a
momentum tilt into the top 4 names by blended 63/126-day momentum, trend-gated)
returned +231% against SPY's +74% on that narrow universe and was promoted. Run
UNCHANGED on this broad universe over the same dates, same data source, same
parameters, it returns +71.6% against SPY's +76.9% -- it loses. The entire edge
was the ticker list. Momentum ranking inside a basket of megacap tech selects
NVDA, and NVDA carried 34.5% of the profit. Do not rediscover that trick: here
there is no basket that only goes up, and any strategy leaning on one name will
be visible in the review as concentration.

THE BAR, AND IT IS LOWER THAN YOU ARE USED TO. Buy-and-hold SPY over 2005-2026
returned +830.8% at Sharpe 0.656 with a -55.2% max drawdown. That Sharpe is
genuinely beatable -- unlike the 1.36 SPY posted in the 2021-2026 tech window --
and the -55% drawdown is the real opportunity. Something that captures most of
the return with materially less drawdown would be a real result.

BUT DO NOT JUST DE-LEVER. Holding 60% SPY and 40% cash also cuts the drawdown,
and it is not a strategy. Watch `oos_exposure`: if your Sharpe improves only
because you are half invested, you have found leverage reduction, not skill. The
test is whether you beat SPY per unit of risk AT COMPARABLE EXPOSURE, or capture
clearly more return at the same risk.

SIZE TO THE LIMITS RATHER THAN FIGHTING THEM. The risk gate here allows at most
20% in any one name, 20 positions, 6 names and 40% of the book per sector, and
1.0 gross. A strategy asking for 25% slots gets every intent clipped to 20%, and
then what was backtested is the gate's version of it rather than yours -- check
`gate.share_clipped_or_blocked` in a review and treat anything above ~50% as a
reason to resize, not as a result. For reference, stock `momo` with its default
4 x 25% slots comes back 94% clipped here; the same idea at 5 x 20% would not.

THE REAL BAR IS NOT SPY. Equal-weight buy-and-hold across these 36 names returned
+933.8% at Sharpe 0.798 with 77% average exposure, against SPY's +830.8% at
0.656. Cap-weighting is the thing to beat only if you cannot beat equal-weight,
so measure yourself against both -- `full_return_vs_market` compares you to SPY,
and experiment n=1 will give you the equal-weight number.

2008 IS IN-SAMPLE AND THAT IS DELIBERATE. The in-sample window covers 2005-2022,
including a -55% market drawdown. A strategy that cannot survive it will show a
badly negative first sub-period -- check `by_period` before the pooled score.
Note also that this universe is 32 companies that are still large today, so every
firm that actually died in 2008 is missing. Your 2008 numbers are therefore
optimistic by construction; treat a strategy that merely survives it as unproven,
not as proven.

WHAT THE DATA SAYS, measured rather than assumed, on this kind of universe:
- Cross-sectional momentum only has signal at ~126 days. Rank correlation between
  trailing and forward returns is about +0.03 at 126d and indistinguishable from
  zero at 5, 21 and 63 days -- slightly negative at 21. Short lookbacks are noise.
- Blending horizons still beats using 126d alone. Weighting the 126d leg to 100%
  measurably hurt out-of-sample results even though it is the only leg with
  univariate signal.
- Volatility is the most predictable quantity here: trailing 21-day realised vol
  correlates about +0.49 with the next 21 days. Using it to time market exposure
  barely paid; using it to equalise risk across the names you already hold
  improved consistency at the cost of return. Both are worth trying properly.
- Deep drawdowns predicted higher forward returns, but under a 1.0 gross-exposure
  cap you cannot exploit that without holding cash the rest of the time, and the
  drag costs more than the boost. Do not spend runs on it.

YOUR HOLDOUT IS UNUSUALLY GOOD. 4.2 years and about 1,048 bars, giving a Sharpe
standard error near 0.49 -- less than half of what previous sessions had. A gap
of 0.5 Sharpe here is real signal rather than noise, so you can actually
distinguish candidates. Use that: it is the first time on this platform that
ranking has meant much.

Finishing with a well-evidenced "nothing here beats buy-and-hold on a broad
universe" is a real and useful result, and given what the last session turned out
to be, an honest negative is worth more than another universe bet in disguise.
